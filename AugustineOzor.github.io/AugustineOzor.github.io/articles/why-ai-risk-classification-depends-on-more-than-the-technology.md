When organizations assess AI risk, they often start by looking at the technology.

But under the **EU AI Act**, risk classification is driven by something more important:

**How the AI is used.**

The same underlying AI model can have very different risk levels depending on its purpose, data, level of autonomy, and potential impact.

### **Consider These Two Healthcare Use Cases**

🤖 **Staff Copilot**

Used for internal productivity tasks such as drafting, summarization, and research support.

Typically:

✅ Lower impact
 ✅ Limited-risk obligations may apply
 ✅ Governance focuses on acceptable use, data protection, and transparency

🏥 **Clinical Decision Agent**

Used to support clinicians with patient care decisions.

Potentially:

✅ Higher impact on individuals
 ✅ Greater regulatory scrutiny
 ✅ Stronger requirements for oversight, validation, monitoring, and accountability

### **What Can Change the Risk Classification?**

The classification is not fixed.

Risk can increase when AI systems:

🔹 Process sensitive data

🔹 Influence consequential decisions

🔹 Operate with greater autonomy

🔹 Affect health, safety, rights, or access to services

🔹 Lack appropriate human oversight

### **Why This Matters**

Two organizations may use similar AI technology but face very different governance obligations because their use cases are different.

That's why effective AI governance starts with understanding:

✅ Intended use

✅ Data being processed

✅ Decisions being influenced

✅ People being affected

✅ Level of human oversight

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI risk classification is not determined by the model alone.**

It depends on what the system does, how it is used, and the impact it can have on people.

**Same technology. Different use case. Different risk.**
