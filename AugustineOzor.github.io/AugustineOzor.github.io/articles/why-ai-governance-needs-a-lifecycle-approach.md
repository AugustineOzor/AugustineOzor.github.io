One of the biggest mistakes organizations make is treating AI governance as a **single approval step**.

In reality, AI governance should span the entire lifecycle:

🔹 Planning & Design
 🔹 Data Collection & Processing
 🔹 Model Selection or Development
 🔹 Testing, Evaluation & Validation
 🔹 Deployment
 🔹 Monitoring & Maintenance
 🔹 Retirement & Decommissioning

Each stage brings its own decisions, risks, controls, and evidence requirements.

A lifecycle approach helps organizations move beyond questions like *"Is this AI approved?"* to more meaningful questions:

✅ What are we building?
 ✅ What data are we using?
 ✅ How was the model tested?
 ✅ Who approved deployment?
 ✅ How is performance being monitored?
 ✅ When should the system be retired?

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI governance is not a one-time approval. It's a repeatable operating model that follows AI from design to retirement.**

The more structured the lifecycle, the easier it becomes to manage risk, demonstrate compliance, and build trust in AI systems.
