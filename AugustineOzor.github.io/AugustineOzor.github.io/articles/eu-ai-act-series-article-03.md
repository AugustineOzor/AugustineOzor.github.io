Article 3 defines the key terms used throughout the EU AI Act. These definitions determine how roles, systems, data, risks and compliance responsibilities are understood.

## Key Requirements

**Key AI actors**: Defines providers, deployers, importers, distributors and other actors.

**AI systems and risk**: Explains core system and risk concepts.

**Data and biometrics**: Covers training data, biometric data, identification and verification.

**Compliance and oversight**: Introduces terms connected with conformity assessment, CE marking and market oversight.

**General-purpose AI**: Defines relevant model concepts and systemic-risk terminology.

## Why Article 3 Matters

Article 3 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

One company develops an AI system, another uses it and a third distributes it. Article 3 helps determine which organization is the provider, deployer or distributor and which obligations follow.

## Key Takeaway

**Article 3 creates the shared language needed for clear accountability.**
