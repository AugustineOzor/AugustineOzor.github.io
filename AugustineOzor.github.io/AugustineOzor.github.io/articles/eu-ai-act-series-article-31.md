Article 31 defines the standards notified bodies must meet. Competence alone is not enough; independence and integrity matter too.

## Key Requirements

**Legal status and resources**: Maintain people, processes, quality systems and cybersecurity.

**Independence**: Remain independent from providers, competitors and financially interested parties.

**Conflicts**: Do not design, develop, market or consult on the same systems being assessed.

**Confidentiality**: Protect sensitive assessment information.

**Qualified personnel**: Maintain technical, legal and assessment expertise.

**Integrity**: Perform work objectively and impartially.

## Why Article 31 Matters

Article 31 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A body that advised a provider on the design of a high-risk system should not later present itself as the independent assessor of that same work.

## Key Takeaway

**A conformity assessment is only as credible as the organization performing it.**
