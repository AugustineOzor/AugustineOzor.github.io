**AI governance does not stop when an AI system is deployed. In many ways, that is when the real work begins.**

One of the most important questions organisations need to ask is:

How is AI actually being used, and where is behaviour starting to drift outside policy?

This is where AI detection and monitoring become critical.

Effective monitoring should provide visibility into:

- **Prompts and outputs:** What are users asking AI systems to do, and what are the systems producing?
- **Users and actions:** Who is interacting with AI, and what actions are being taken as a result?
- **Data access:** Is sensitive, confidential, or restricted information being exposed or accessed inappropriately?
- **Tool usage:** Are AI agents calling tools, APIs, databases, or other systems in ways that align with their intended permissions?

With this visibility, organisations can begin detecting patterns that may indicate misuse or emerging risk, including prompt injection, data leakage, unsafe outputs, hallucinated content, unusual tool calls, policy bypass attempts, model drift, and anomalous activity.

But detection alone is not enough.

An alert has little value if nobody knows who should act on it.

AI monitoring therefore needs to connect with existing security operations, risk management, privacy, legal, compliance, and incident response workflows.

In practice, the lifecycle should be simple:

**Monitor → Detect → Investigate → Escalate → Respond → Learn**

## Key takeaway

**You cannot effectively govern AI that you cannot see.**

Policies define the boundaries. Monitoring tells us whether AI activity is actually staying within them.

As organisations move from experimenting with AI to deploying AI systems and agents at scale, continuous visibility should become a core part of responsible AI governance.
