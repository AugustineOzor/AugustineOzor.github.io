**Not every AI system creates the same security risks. The risks depend heavily on how AI enters the organization.**

A useful way to assess AI risk is to first identify the AI adoption path.

Here are four common paths and the risks to watch:

### BYOAI (Bring Your Own AI)
Employees use external or personal AI tools for work. Key concerns include:

- Sensitive information entered into prompts or retained in memory
- Limited organizational access controls
- Untrusted or unverifiable outputs being used for business decisions

### Embedded AI
AI capabilities are built into existing enterprise applications. The risks can include:

- AI features introducing unexpected data exposure
- Limited visibility or auditability of AI actions
- Shadow AI and third-party model or supply chain risks

### Built AI
The organization develops or operates its own AI solution. This introduces risks such as:

- Contaminated or poorly governed training data
- Unhardened AI runtime environments
- Weak versioning, lifecycle management, or rollback controls

### Blended AI
AI is connected to enterprise data, tools, APIs, agents, and workflows. Here the risk shifts toward autonomy:

- Untrusted context or sensitive data being synthesized
- Excessive agent permissions and tool access
- Untraceable action chains and cascading failures

The important point is that AI risk is architecture-dependent.

A BYOAI tool, an embedded copilot, an internally developed model, and an autonomous agent should not automatically receive the same security assessment.

A simple discovery question can help:

“How is this AI system being adopted, and have the risks specific to that path been addressed?”

This turns the adoption-path model into a practical checklist for AI inventory, risk assessment, security architecture, and governance reviews.

## Key takeaway

**Know the AI adoption path before deciding which controls are needed.**
