When people talk about AI adoption, it's easy to think of AI as one big category.

In reality, AI is entering organizations through **three common paths**, and each comes with different governance, security, and risk management challenges.

### **🌐 Public AI**

This is AI that employees access directly through public services such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or GitHub Copilot.

✅ Fast and accessible
 ✅ Boosts productivity and creativity

However, it also creates concerns around:

🔹 Sensitive data exposure
 🔹 Shadow AI usage
 🔹 Unknown retention and training practices
 🔹 Limited organizational control

### **☁️ Embedded AI**

AI capabilities integrated into platforms organizations already use, such as:

🔹 Microsoft 365 Copilot
 🔹 Salesforce Einstein
 🔹 Snowflake Cortex

The focus shifts from development to **configuration, governance, and access management**.

Key questions include:

✅ Who can access it?
 ✅ What data can it see?
 ✅ Are permissions properly configured?

### **⚙️ Homegrown AI**

These are AI solutions built internally, including:

🔹 Enterprise chatbots
 🔹 RAG applications
 🔹 AI agents
 🔹 Custom AI workflows

This approach offers the greatest flexibility and business value, but also introduces more responsibility.

Organizations must manage:

🔹 Data pipelines
 🔹 Model behavior
 🔹 Prompt security
 🔹 Monitoring and observability
 🔹 Ongoing governance

### **Why This Matters**

The controls needed for AI depend heavily on **where the AI runs, who owns it, what data it accesses, and how it is used.**

A public AI tool, an embedded AI feature, and a custom AI agent may all create value, but they require different governance strategies.

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI governance is not one-size-fits-all.**

To manage AI effectively, organizations first need to understand how AI is being adopted across the enterprise and apply controls that match each adoption path.

**The question isn't just what AI can do. It's where it's running, who is using it, and how quickly it's spreading across your environment.**
