When organizations think about AI risk, the conversation often starts with technology. But for **Public AI tools** such as ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub Copilot, and Notion AI, governance is often the most important control.

Why?

Because organizations usually **do not control the underlying model, infrastructure, or code path**. Employees can often sign up and start using these tools in minutes, sometimes with personal accounts and without any formal approval process.

This means managing Public AI requires more than security tools. It requires clear governance.

### **🔹 OWASP Top 10 for LLMs**

OWASP highlights several risks that are highly relevant to Public AI:

✅ Prompt Injection
 ✅ Excessive Agency
 ✅ Training Data Leakage

These risks reinforce the need for clear guidance on what employees can and cannot share with external AI systems.

### **🔹 NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**

NIST reminds us that governance begins with accountability.

Organizations should define:

✅ Who can use Public AI tools
 ✅ Which tools are approved
 ✅ What data can be shared
 ✅ What activities are prohibited
 ✅ How incidents are reported and escalated

Without ownership and accountability, AI adoption can quickly become unmanaged.

### **🔹 ISO/IEC 42001**

ISO 42001 emphasizes the importance of:

✅ Defined roles and responsibilities
 ✅ Formal approval processes
 ✅ Ongoing oversight
 ✅ Documented governance practices

This helps prevent shadow AI from becoming accepted practice without appropriate controls.

### **Why This Matters**

Many organizations focus on securing AI technology.

But one of the most effective controls is often much simpler:

📌 A clear policy
 📌 Defined responsibilities
 📌 Employee training
 📌 Management oversight

Technology helps. Governance makes it sustainable.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Public AI should not operate outside governance simply because it operates outside your infrastructure.**

Frameworks such as **OWASP Top 10 for LLMs, NIST AI RMF, and ISO/IEC 42001** provide a structured way to define:

🔹 Who may use Public AI
 🔹 What they can do with it
 🔹 How risks are monitored and managed
 🔹 Who is accountable when something goes wrong

**The most powerful AI control is often not a security tool. It's a well-defined and well-enforced governance program.**
