Article 8 connects high-risk classification to the substantive requirements that follow. Compliance must reflect how the AI system is actually designed, tested, operated and monitored.

## Key Requirements

**Operational reality**: Documentation alone is insufficient if controls do not work in practice.

**Risk management**: Compliance should be supported by structured lifecycle risk management.

**Existing regulation**: AI requirements can be integrated into existing regulated-product compliance processes.

**Avoid duplication**: Testing, reporting and documentation may be combined where appropriate without reducing accountability.

**Lifecycle approach**: Compliance must remain current as the system changes.

## Why Article 8 Matters

Article 8 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A medical-device manufacturer can integrate AI risk, testing and documentation into its existing medical-device quality and compliance system.

## Key Takeaway

**Compliance is not paperwork. It is evidence that the AI system is governed effectively.**
