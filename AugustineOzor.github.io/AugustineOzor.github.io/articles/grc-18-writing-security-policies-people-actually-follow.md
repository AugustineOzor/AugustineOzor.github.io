Policies are where governance becomes visible to the whole organisation. Too often they are long, vague and ignored.

## Principles for effective policies

- **Keep them short.** State the requirement and the reason. Move technical detail into standards and procedures.
- **Write for the reader.** Use plain language and active voice: "Staff must lock their screens when away from their desk."
- **Make requirements testable.** "Access is reviewed quarterly by the system owner" can be audited; "access is reviewed regularly" cannot.
- **Assign ownership.** Each policy needs an owner responsible for keeping it current.
- **Explain consequences and exceptions.** State what happens on non-compliance and how to request an approved exception.
- **Review on a schedule,** typically every year, and after major incidents or regulatory change.

## Getting policies adopted

Publishing a policy is not the same as implementing it. Pair new policies with short training, clear guidance and, where possible, technical enforcement, so the secure option is also the easiest one.

## Common policy set

Most programmes start with an information security policy, acceptable use, access control, data classification and handling, incident response, business continuity and third-party risk. More specialised policies, such as acceptable use of generative AI, are now becoming standard too.

**Key takeaway:** A good policy is short, testable and owned, and is backed by training and controls that make it easy to follow.
