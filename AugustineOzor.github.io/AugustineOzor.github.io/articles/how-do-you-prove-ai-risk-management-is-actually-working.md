Having AI governance processes is important.

**Being able to prove they're working is even more important.**

That's where **evidence** comes in.

For AI risk management to be effective, organizations should be able to demonstrate:

✅ **AI inventories and use-case records**
 Showing where AI is being used and who is accountable for it.

✅ **Risk and impact assessments**
 Showing that risks, potential harms, stakeholders, and regulatory obligations have been evaluated.

✅ **Risk classification decisions**
 Showing why a system was classified as low, medium, or high risk and why the selected controls are appropriate.

✅ **Control mapping and testing evidence**
 Showing that controls were implemented, tested, and validated.

✅ **Residual risk approvals**
 Showing that leadership understood and formally accepted the risks that remain after controls are applied.

✅ **Monitoring and usage evidence**
 Showing that AI systems are being monitored and that unusual activity or policy violations can be detected.

### **Why This Matters**

Many organizations believe governance exists because they have policies.

Auditors, regulators, and leadership typically look for something more:

**Evidence.**

If risk decisions cannot be demonstrated, governance becomes difficult to verify.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Without evidence, governance is assumed. With evidence, governance is proven.**

Strong AI governance creates a clear record of how risks were assessed, how decisions were made, and how compliance is maintained over time.

**Good governance manages risk. Great governance can prove it.**
