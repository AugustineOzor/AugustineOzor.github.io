One of the most important questions in the EU AI Act is: **"Is my AI system high-risk?"** Article 6 provides the answer by defining the rules used to classify AI systems as **high-risk**.

Why does this matter? Once an AI system is classified as high-risk, it must meet stricter requirements for **risk management, transparency, documentation, human oversight, and compliance.**

**Two Main Pathways to High-Risk Classification**

🔹 **Safety Components of Regulated Products**
 An AI system is considered high-risk if it is part of a product that already requires safety assessments under EU legislation, such as certain medical devices, vehicles, or machinery.

🔹 **High-Risk Use Cases (Annex III)**
 AI systems used in sensitive areas may also be classified as high-risk, including:

- Biometric identification

- Critical infrastructure

- Education and training

- Employment and recruitment

- Access to essential services

- Law enforcement

- Migration and border control

- Justice and democratic processes

**Important Exception**

Not every AI system used in these areas is automatically high-risk. If the system performs only a **limited procedural or supporting task**, does not significantly influence decisions, and does not create substantial risks to people, it may qualify for an exception.

However, providers must be able to **document and justify** that decision.

An AI tool that simply organizes job applications alphabetically may not be considered high-risk. However, if it ranks candidates, predicts job performance, or influences hiring decisions, it is likely to be classified as high-risk.

**Key Takeaway**

**Article 6 is the gateway to AI compliance.** It helps organizations determine whether an AI system falls into the high-risk category and whether additional governance and regulatory requirements apply.

**The higher the impact on people's rights, opportunities, or safety, the greater the need for oversight.**
