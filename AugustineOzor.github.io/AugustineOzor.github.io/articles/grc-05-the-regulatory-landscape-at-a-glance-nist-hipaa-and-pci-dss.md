Frameworks and regulations differ in who they bind and how much choice they leave. Three appear in almost every early GRC role.

## NIST

The US National Institute of Standards and Technology publishes widely used, voluntary guidance (mandatory for US federal systems):

- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0** organises security outcomes into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover.
- **NIST SP 800-53** is a detailed catalogue of security and privacy controls.
- **NIST SP 800-37**, the Risk Management Framework (RMF), describes how to select, implement, assess and authorise those controls for a system.

## HIPAA

The US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act protects health information. Its **Security Rule** requires administrative, physical and technical safeguards for electronic protected health information (ePHI). It applies to covered entities (such as healthcare providers and insurers) and their business associates. The **Breach Notification Rule** sets duties when ePHI is exposed.

## PCI DSS

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard is a **contractual** requirement for any organisation that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data. Version 4.0 sets out 12 requirements, from network security and encryption to access control, logging and regular testing.

## How they fit together

NIST provides the method and the control catalogue. HIPAA and PCI DSS define obligations for particular kinds of data. Many organisations implement NIST-style controls once and map them to the specific regulations they must meet.

**Key takeaway:** Know which rules bind you, then build one control set that satisfies them all.
