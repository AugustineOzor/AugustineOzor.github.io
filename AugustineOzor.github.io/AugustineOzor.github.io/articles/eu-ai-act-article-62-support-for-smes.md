# EU AI Act Article 62: Compliance Should Not Become a Barrier to Innovation

*By Augustine Ozor*

**The EU AI Act is not only about regulating AI. It also recognises that compliance should not become a barrier to innovation, especially for SMEs and start-ups.**

While reading Article 62 of the EU AI Act, one thing stood out to me: smaller organisations often do not have the same legal, technical and compliance resources as larger companies.

Article 62 addresses this by introducing measures to support SMEs, start-ups and other smaller operators.

Some key measures include:

- Priority access to AI regulatory sandboxes for eligible SMEs and start-ups to develop and test AI systems in a supervised environment.
- Tailored training and awareness to help smaller organisations understand their obligations under the EU AI Act.
- Dedicated communication channels where SMEs and start-ups can seek practical regulatory guidance.
- Participation in standardisation, giving smaller businesses a voice in shaping the standards that may affect them.
- Reduced conformity assessment fees, taking factors such as the size and market size of SMEs into account.

The AI Office also has an important role in providing standardised templates, accessible information and awareness initiatives to make compliance easier to navigate.

For me, the bigger message is simple:

Responsible AI regulation should protect people without pushing smaller innovators out of the market.

The rules matter, but so does the ability of businesses to understand, afford and implement them.

Finding the right balance between innovation, responsible AI and proportionate compliance will be key.

> **Update (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force since July 2026):** The Omnibus extended several SME measures, including priority access to regulatory sandboxes, to a new category of small mid-cap enterprises. Check the consolidated Regulation for the current wording.

> **Question for the reader:** What do you think? Will these measures be enough to help SMEs and start-ups navigate AI regulation while continuing to innovate?
