Article 25 explains when another actor may become the provider of an AI system and inherit provider obligations.

## Key Requirements

**Own name or trademark**: Rebranding a high-risk system can shift responsibility.

**Substantial modification**: A significant change may make the modifier the provider.

**Changed intended purpose**: Repurposing a system can move it into a high-risk use.

**Third-party cooperation**: Original providers and component suppliers may have information and cooperation duties.

**Compliance impact**: Risk, documentation, conformity and monitoring obligations may shift.

**Change governance**: Assess regulatory impact before modifying, rebranding or repurposing.

## Why Article 25 Matters

Article 25 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A scheduling assistant is modified to rank job applicants. That intended-purpose change may create high-risk obligations and a new provider role.

## Key Takeaway

**Changing the AI may also change who is accountable for it.**
