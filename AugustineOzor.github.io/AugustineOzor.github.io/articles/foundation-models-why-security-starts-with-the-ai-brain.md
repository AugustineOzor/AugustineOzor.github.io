When organizations build AI solutions, most people focus on chatbots, copilots, or applications. But the real starting point is the **foundation model**, the core intelligence that powers the entire system.

Models such as **GPT, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA** act as the "brain" of an AI system. Everything the AI generates is influenced by how that model was trained and how it is deployed.

From a security and governance perspective, this matters more than many organizations realize.

🔹 **What data was used to train the model?**
 Biased, copyrighted, or sensitive data can introduce risks such as inaccurate outputs, hallucinations, and reputational damage.

🔹 **Where is the model hosted?**
 Externally hosted models create data-sharing, privacy, and compliance considerations that must be carefully managed.

🔹 **Who controls the model?**
 Third-party models require vendor oversight, contractual safeguards, and clear governance controls.

🔹 **How is data protected?**
 Organizations should implement controls such as data redaction, logging, monitoring, and prompt filtering to reduce exposure risks.

🔹 **What happens if you host the model yourself?**
 Self-hosting provides more control and transparency, but also increases responsibility for security, updates, and model management.

### **Why It Matters**

The foundation model is more than a technology choice. It is a **security, governance, and risk management decision**.

Before deploying AI, ask:

✅ What model are we using?
 ✅ Where is it hosted?
 ✅ What was it trained on?
 ✅ Who controls it?
 ✅ What risks does it introduce?

**Key Takeaway:**
 Strong AI governance starts with understanding the foundation model powering your AI system. The decisions made at this layer influence every output, risk, and control that follows.

**Secure AI starts with a secure foundation.**
