Controls do not run themselves. Behind every security programme is a set of roles, and many GRC problems trace back to a role that nobody owns.

## The core roles

- **Senior leadership and the board** set risk appetite, fund the programme and are ultimately accountable.
- **The CISO or security lead** turns that direction into a programme: strategy, policy, priorities and reporting.
- **The system owner** is accountable for a specific system, its budget and its risks. In a hospital, this might be the head of clinical systems for the medical records platform.
- **The information system security officer (ISSO)** handles day-to-day security for that system: documentation, control status and monitoring.
- **Data owners and custodians** decide how data is classified and used (owners) and keep it safe in practice (custodians, often IT).
- **The authorising official (AO)** is the senior person who formally accepts the remaining risk and approves the system to operate.
- **Assessors and auditors** independently test whether controls work. Independence matters: the person who built a control should not be the one who signs it off.

## Why this matters

Clear roles make accountability visible. When an access review is missed or a vulnerability goes unpatched, a well-run programme can say exactly who was responsible and who should have escalated it.

A simple **RACI matrix** (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) for each key activity is one of the most useful documents a new programme can create.

**Key takeaway:** Every control needs a named owner. If everyone is responsible, nobody is.
