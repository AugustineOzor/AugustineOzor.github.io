# Six Steps to a Gap Assessment That Survives Stage 1

*By Augustine Ozor · 6 min read*

A gap assessment is only useful if you can defend it. The version that survives a certification body's Stage 1 is evidence-first, rated conservatively and written so that a re-read months later still rings true. Here are six steps, with realistic time estimates for a small company, that get you there.

| Step | What you do | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fix the scope of the assessment | 15 min |
| 2 | Break Clauses 4 to 10 into assessable rows | 30 min |
| 3 | Gather evidence before you rate | 2 to 3 days |
| 4 | Rate on the 3-tier scale, conservatively | Half a day |
| 5 | Total and prioritize | 1 hour |
| 6 | Write the Gap Analysis Report | Half a day |

In total that is roughly 3.5 to 4.5 working days. The time depends on the organization's size and how quickly owners can produce evidence, so treat these figures as a planning guide rather than a promise.

## Step 1 · Fix the scope of the assessment (15 min)

Write one line at the top of the document:

> This assessment covers ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clauses 4 to 10. Annex A controls are out of scope and will be assessed via risk treatment and the Statement of Applicability.

This single sentence stops the assessment sprawling into a 93-control review, and it tells every reader exactly what they are looking at.

## Step 2 · Break Clauses 4 to 10 into assessable rows (30 min)

One rating per whole clause is too coarse. Break each clause into its sub-requirements (4.1, 4.2, 4.3; 5.1, 5.2, 5.3; and so on) so that each row is small enough to rate honestly. Aim for roughly 15 to 25 rows for a small company.

A useful row layout:

| Clause | Requirement (short) | Evidence I would demand | Evidence seen | Rating | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.2 | Information security policy | Signed, current policy | | | |

## Step 3 · Gather evidence before you rate (2 to 3 days)

For each row, ask the owner one question: "Show me the document or record that proves this." Read what they hand you. The rating comes after the evidence, never before. If nobody can show you anything, that is a finding, not a delay.

> **The one question that does the work.** "What would you show an auditor for this?"
> - A real artifact you can read: lean **Compliant**.
> - "We sort of do that" or "it's in someone's head": **Partial** at best.
> - Silence: **Non-Compliant**.
>
> The evidence question turns opinion into assessment.

## Step 4 · Rate on the 3-tier scale, conservatively (half a day)

Apply Compliant, Partially Compliant or Non-Compliant. When you genuinely cannot decide between two tiers, choose the lower one. An honest understatement costs a conversation; an overstatement costs your credibility at Stage 1. Record, for every row, the evidence you would still need to obtain.

## Step 5 · Total and prioritize (1 hour)

Count the ratings. Then order the remediation:

1. **Non-Compliant first.** These block certification outright, and several are prerequisites for everything else: no scope, no risk assessment; no policy, no direction.
2. **Partial second.** These need finishing, not building.

Sequence the fixes along the implementation order you already know, so the plan follows the dependencies rather than whoever shouts loudest.

## Step 6 · Write the Gap Analysis Report (half a day)

Assemble the seven sections: Purpose, Scope, Methodology, Findings table, Totals, Prioritized recommendations and Conclusion. Keep the headline honest. The report is both the funding case and the yardstick you will be measured against later, so write it so that a re-read in eight months still rings true.

## If you run all six steps

Leadership gets a real number instead of a feeling. You get a prioritized work plan that falls straight out of the evidence you couldn't be shown. And Stage 1 becomes a confirmation rather than a discovery. That is what a gap assessment is for.
