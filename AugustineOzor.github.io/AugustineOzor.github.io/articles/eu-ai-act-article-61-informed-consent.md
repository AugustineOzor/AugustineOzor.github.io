# EU AI Act Article 61: What Does Informed Consent Look Like When AI Is Tested in the Real World?

*By Augustine Ozor*

One part of the EU AI Act that caught my attention is Article 61, which focuses on informed consent when certain AI systems are tested in real-world conditions outside AI regulatory sandboxes.

The principle is straightforward: people should understand what they are agreeing to before participating in AI testing.

Article 61 requires freely given informed consent before participation. The information provided should be concise, clear, relevant and understandable.

Participants should be informed about:

- The nature and purpose of the testing, including what the AI system is being tested for and any possible inconvenience.
- The conditions and expected duration, so participants understand what participation involves and how long it may last.
- Their rights, including the right to refuse to participate or to withdraw at any time without negative consequences or having to justify the decision.
- AI decisions, including arrangements for requesting that predictions, recommendations or decisions made by the AI system be reversed or disregarded.
- Identification and contact information, so participants know who is responsible for the testing and where to obtain further information.

And importantly, consent cannot simply be an informal "yes."

It must be dated, documented, and a copy provided to the participant or legal representative.

A simple example would be testing a high-risk AI system used to assess loan applications.

Before applicants participate in that real-world testing, they should clearly understand what is being tested, how their participation works, their rights, and what they can do about an AI-generated recommendation affecting them.

For me, Article 61 highlights an important point:

Responsible AI is not only about building systems that work. It is also about making sure people understand when and how those systems are being tested on them.

Innovation matters. But so do transparency, choice and human rights.

No informed consent, no participation.
