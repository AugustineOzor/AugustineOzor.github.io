One of the biggest challenges in AI governance is that **AI risks are often discussed in different places and different ways.**

One framework focuses on bias.

Another focuses on privacy.

Another focuses on security or safety.

This can make it difficult for organizations to build a complete picture of AI risk.

That's where the **MIT AI Risk Repository** is valuable.

It provides a structured taxonomy of AI risks, bringing together a wide range of documented risks into a common framework.

### **What Makes It Useful?**

✅ Helps identify governance blind spots

✅ Provides a common language for discussing AI risk

✅ Distinguishes between **types of risk** and **causes of risk**

✅ Supports more consistent risk assessments

Organizations can use it to evaluate whether they are considering risks such as:

🔹 Bias and discrimination

🔹 Privacy leakage

🔹 Harmful or misleading content

🔹 Security vulnerabilities

🔹 Model misuse

🔹 System failures

🔹 Misinformation

### **Why This Matters**

Effective AI governance starts with understanding what could go wrong.

If teams use different terms, categories, or definitions, important risks can be overlooked.

A structured risk taxonomy helps organizations assess, prioritize, and manage AI risks more consistently.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Mature AI governance requires more than a list of risks. It requires a shared risk vocabulary.**

The better organizations can classify and discuss AI risks, the better they can manage them.

**You can't effectively govern risks that you haven't clearly defined.**
