Many organizations are already using AI. The real challenge is no longer **whether AI is being adopted**, but **how to govern it safely and responsibly**.

In most enterprises, AI adoption typically follows three paths:

🌐 **Public AI**
 Tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and GitHub Copilot that employees access directly.

☁️ **Embedded AI**
 AI capabilities built into platforms already used across the business, such as Microsoft 365 Copilot, Salesforce Einstein, and Snowflake Cortex.

⚙️ **Homegrown AI**
 Custom chatbots, RAG solutions, AI agents, and applications developed internally.

Each path creates different opportunities, but also different risks.

A public AI tool may introduce concerns about sensitive information being shared outside organizational control.

An embedded AI solution requires careful configuration, permissions management, and data access controls.

A homegrown AI application introduces additional responsibilities around data pipelines, security, monitoring, model behavior, and lifecycle governance.

### **The Next Step**

Understanding AI adoption is only the beginning.

Organizations need to understand:

🔹 The risks associated with each AI adoption path
 🔹 How those risks impact people, data, and business processes
 🔹 Which frameworks and regulations apply
 🔹 What governance and security controls should be implemented

This is where frameworks such as:

✅ **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**
 ✅ **ISO/IEC 42001**
 ✅ **EU AI Act**
 ✅ **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications**

become increasingly important.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Successful AI adoption is not about blocking innovation. It's about enabling AI safely, responsibly, and at scale.**

The organizations that succeed with AI will be those that understand not only what AI can do, but also how to manage the risks that come with it.
