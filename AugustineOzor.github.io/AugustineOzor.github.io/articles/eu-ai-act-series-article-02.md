Article 2 defines the scope of the EU AI Act: who must follow the rules and when they apply. The key message is that the Act is not only for organizations based in Europe. If an AI system is placed on the EU market, used in the EU or produces outputs used in the EU, the regulation may apply.

## Key Requirements

**Providers**: Organizations placing AI systems or models on the EU market.

**Deployers**: Organizations using AI systems within the EU.

**Organizations outside the EU**: Non-EU providers or deployers may be covered when outputs are used in the EU.

**Importers and distributors**: Organizations bringing AI systems into or making them available on the EU market.

**Product manufacturers**: Organizations embedding AI in regulated products.

**Authorized representatives**: EU-based representatives acting for certain non-EU providers.

**Exclusions**: Certain military, national-security, research and open-source activities may be treated differently under specific conditions.

## Why Article 2 Matters

Article 2 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A provider in the United States supplies an AI recruitment tool used by a business in Germany. Even though the provider is outside the EU, the system may fall within scope because it is used in the EU.

## Key Takeaway

**AI has no borders. Responsible AI governance should not either.**
