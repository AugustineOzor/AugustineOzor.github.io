Article 36 addresses relevant changes to notified bodies and what happens if a body stops operating or its notification changes.

## Key Requirements

**Report changes**: Important changes must remain visible to the notifying authority.

**Scope changes**: Expanded or altered scope follows the relevant process.

**Cessation**: Authorities and affected providers must be informed.

**Records**: Assessment files remain protected and accessible.

**Certificates**: Existing certificates may require review.

**Continuity**: Responsibilities may need transfer to another qualified body.

## Why Article 36 Matters

Article 36 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

If a notified body leaves the market, open files and certificates require controlled review and continuity arrangements rather than simply disappearing.

## Key Takeaway

**Trustworthy oversight must continue even when organizations change.**
