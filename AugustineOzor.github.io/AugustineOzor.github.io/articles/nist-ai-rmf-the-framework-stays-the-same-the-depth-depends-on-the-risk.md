One of the strengths of the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)** is that it can be applied to very different AI systems.

The framework doesn't change.

**The level of governance does.**

Consider two healthcare AI use cases:

🔹 **Staff Copilot** used for drafting, summarization, and internal productivity

🔹 **Clinical Decision Agent** used to support patient care decisions

Both use AI.

Both require governance.

But they don't require the same level of oversight.

### **Govern**

For a Staff Copilot, governance may focus on:

✅ Acceptable use policies
 ✅ Vendor reviews
 ✅ Ownership and accountability

For a Clinical Decision Agent, governance expands to include:

✅ Clinical oversight
 ✅ Approval rights
 ✅ Patient safety accountability

### **Map**

For a Staff Copilot:

✅ Users
 ✅ Data categories
 ✅ Approved use cases
 ✅ Vendor dependencies

For a Clinical Decision Agent:

✅ Patient impact
 ✅ Clinical workflows
 ✅ Data flows
 ✅ Affected groups and context

### **Measure**

For a Staff Copilot:

✅ Data leakage risk
 ✅ Output quality
 ✅ Policy violations

For a Clinical Decision Agent:

✅ Accuracy
 ✅ Bias
 ✅ Safety
 ✅ Model drift
 ✅ Human oversight effectiveness

### **Manage**

For a Staff Copilot:

✅ DLP controls
 ✅ Monitoring
 ✅ User coaching
 ✅ Exception handling

For a Clinical Decision Agent:

✅ Escalation procedures
 ✅ Risk containment
 ✅ Residual risk reviews
 ✅ Revalidation and ongoing monitoring

### **Key Takeaway**

**The NIST AI RMF provides a consistent structure for managing AI risk, but governance should always be proportional to the use case.**

A productivity assistant and a clinical decision-support system should not be governed the same way.

The higher the potential impact on people, the deeper the governance needs to be.

**Same framework. Different risk. Different depth.**
