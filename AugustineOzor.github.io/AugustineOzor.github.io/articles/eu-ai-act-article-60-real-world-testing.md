# EU AI Act Article 60: Can High-Risk AI Be Tested in the Real World?

*By Augustine Ozor*

Testing AI in a controlled environment is important. But some risks only become visible when an AI system interacts with real people, real processes, and real-world conditions.

That is where Article 60 of the EU AI Act comes in. It allows certain high-risk AI systems to be tested in real-world conditions outside an AI regulatory sandbox before they are placed on the market or put into service. But this is not uncontrolled experimentation. Strict safeguards apply.

## What does Article 60 require?

- **A real-world testing plan:** The provider must define how the test will work and submit the plan to the relevant market-surveillance authority.
- **Regulatory oversight:** The testing must follow the required authorisation and registration process.
- **Clear accountability:** Where providers and deployers work together, their roles and responsibilities must be clearly defined.
- **Protection of participants:** Vulnerable groups, including people affected because of age or disability, require appropriate safeguards.
- **Informed consent:** Participants generally need to provide informed consent, subject to the specific exceptions provided by the Act.
- **Qualified human oversight:** Testing must be supervised by people with the appropriate expertise, training, capacity, and authority.
- **Reversibility:** AI-generated predictions, recommendations, or decisions must be capable of being reversed or disregarded.

## What happens if something goes wrong?

Real-world testing requires organisations to be ready to act. If a serious incident occurs, providers may need to:

- Report it to the relevant authority.
- Take immediate mitigation measures.
- Suspend testing where necessary.
- Terminate the test if the risk cannot be controlled.

Participants must be able to withdraw from testing without detriment. Providers may also remain responsible for damage caused during real-world testing under applicable liability laws.

## Why this matters

Article 60 recognises that AI cannot always be fully evaluated in a laboratory. Real-world testing can reveal risks, limitations, and behaviours that controlled environments may miss.

But real-world experimentation should never mean uncontrolled experimentation.

## Key takeaway

Article 60 is about learning from the real world without removing accountability. Test in reality. Protect people. Monitor continuously. Be ready to stop.

Innovation needs experimentation, but responsible experimentation needs guardrails.

> **Update (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force since July 2026):** The Omnibus also amended provisions on testing AI systems in real-world conditions. Check the consolidated Regulation for the current wording.
