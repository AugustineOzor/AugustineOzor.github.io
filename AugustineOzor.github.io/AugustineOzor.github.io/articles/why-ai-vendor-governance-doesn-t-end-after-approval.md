When organizations talk about AI governance, the focus is often on internal systems.

But AI risk doesn't stop at the enterprise boundary.

Many organizations rely on:

✅ SaaS providers
 ✅ AI model providers
 ✅ Cloud platforms
 ✅ Data processors
 ✅ Integration partners

And each can introduce new risks.

### **Start With Due Diligence**

Before approving a vendor, organizations should understand:

🔹 How the AI works
 🔹 What data it accesses
 🔹 How data is handled and protected
 🔹 The vendor's security posture
 🔹 Compliance and regulatory claims
 🔹 Incident response capabilities

### **Strengthen Contracts**

Good governance should also be reflected in contracts.

Important controls may include:

✅ Audit rights
 ✅ Data-use restrictions
 ✅ Transparency requirements
 ✅ Incident reporting obligations
 ✅ Security requirements
 ✅ Retention terms
 ✅ Restrictions on model training or data reuse

### **Keep Monitoring**

The biggest mistake organizations make is treating vendor review as a one-time exercise.

Vendors evolve.

Models change.

Features are added.

Integrations expand.

Regulatory expectations shift.

Continuous monitoring helps organizations understand when those changes create new risks.

### **Evidence Matters**

A mature third-party AI governance process should maintain:

✅ Supplier assessments
 ✅ Risk ratings
 ✅ Approval records
 ✅ Contractual evidence
 ✅ Monitoring activities
 ✅ Reassessment schedules

### **Key Takeaway**

**Initial vendor approval is the beginning, not the end, of AI governance.**

As AI capabilities evolve, organizations need ongoing oversight to ensure third-party risks remain understood, managed, and accountable.

**You can outsource a service. You can't outsource accountability.**
