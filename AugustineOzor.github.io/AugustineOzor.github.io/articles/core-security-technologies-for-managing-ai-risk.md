As AI adoption grows, traditional security controls alone are no longer enough.

AI introduces new challenges around data, models, prompts, agents, and automated decision-making. To manage these risks effectively, organizations need security capabilities specifically designed for AI.

There are **eight core security technologies** that form the foundation of a modern AI security program.

### **🔍 Discover & Govern**

**Data Security & Posture Management (DSPM)**
 Helps organizations discover, classify, and protect sensitive data used in AI systems, including prompts, training data, RAG indexes, and AI workflows.

**AI Security & Posture Management (AISPM)**
 Provides visibility into AI systems, models, agents, and copilots. It helps organizations understand where AI is being used, who owns it, and whether it aligns with approved policies.

### **🛡️ Monitor & Protect**

**Identity & Access Management (IAM)**
 Ensures only authorized users, applications, and agents can access AI systems and data.

**AI Firewalls, DLP & Gateways**
 Provide real-time protection by monitoring prompts and responses, detecting prompt injection attempts, preventing data leakage, and enforcing AI usage policies.

**AI Detection & Response (AI-DR)**
 Monitors the entire AI interaction chain, from prompts and retrieved data to model outputs and agent actions, helping detect misuse, abnormal behavior, and security incidents.

### **✅ Recover & Trust**

**Audit Trails & Lineage**
 Create visibility and accountability by recording AI activity, decisions, approvals, changes, and policy exceptions.

**Data Integrity & Provenance**
 Help organizations understand where data came from, whether it can be trusted, and whether it has been altered, poisoned, or used improperly.

**AI Red Teaming**
 Continuously tests AI systems against real-world threats such as jailbreaks, prompt injection, data exfiltration, and unsafe agent behavior.

### **Why This Matters**

Whether you're using **Public AI, Embedded AI, or Homegrown AI**, governance starts with visibility and trust.

Organizations need to know:

✅ What data AI can access
 ✅ Who can use AI systems
 ✅ How AI behaves in production
 ✅ Whether controls are working as intended
 ✅ How to demonstrate compliance and accountability

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI security is not a single tool. It is a collection of capabilities working together.**

DSPM and AISPM help you discover and govern risk.

IAM, AI Firewalls, DLP, Gateways, and AI Detection & Response help protect AI systems in real time.

Audit Trails, Data Provenance, and AI Red Teaming help organizations build trust, demonstrate compliance, and continuously improve resilience.

The goal isn't just to deploy AI. It's to deploy AI that is secure, governed, and trustworthy.
