**Traditional security controls are essential, but securing AI requires additional capabilities.**

AI systems introduce a different attack surface. Security teams now need visibility not only into networks, identities, and endpoints, but also into prompts, outputs, models, retrieval sources, agents, tools, and AI-driven actions.

A practical AI security control model can focus on 8 key capabilities:

### 1. Data Security & Posture Management (DSPM)
Discover, classify, and protect sensitive data flowing through AI pipelines, retrieval systems, and prompts.

### 2. AI Security & Posture Management (AISPM)
Maintain visibility into models, copilots, agents, plugins, and AI services, including where and how AI is being used.

### 3. Identity & Access Management (IAM)
Control who can access, use, modify, or administer AI systems and connected tools.

### 4. AI Firewalls, DLP & Gateways
Inspect prompts and outputs to help prevent sensitive data leakage, prompt injection, unsafe interactions, and unauthorized content.

### 5. AI Detection & Response
Monitor the full prompt-to-action chain to identify suspicious behavior, misuse, data exfiltration, and unsafe AI activity.

### 6. Audit Trails & Lineage
Maintain evidence of AI activity and changes so organizations can understand what happened, trace decisions, investigate incidents, and demonstrate accountability.

### 7. Data Integrity & Provenance
Track where AI data comes from and help identify poor-quality, manipulated, or poisoned data entering AI systems.

### 8. AI Red Teaming
Continuously test AI systems and agents against threats such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage, and adversarial manipulation.

## Key takeaway

**AI security requires more than adding AI to an existing cybersecurity program.**

Organizations need a security control plane that understands how AI behaves, what data it accesses, what it generates, what tools it can use, and what actions it can take.

As AI becomes more agentic and connected to business processes, that visibility becomes increasingly important.

You cannot effectively secure AI if your security controls cannot understand AI behavior.
