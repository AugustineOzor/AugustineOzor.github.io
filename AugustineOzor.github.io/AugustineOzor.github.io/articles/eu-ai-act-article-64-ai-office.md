# EU AI Act Article 64: Good AI Governance Needs More Than Rules

*By Augustine Ozor*

**Good AI governance needs more than rules. It needs the right expertise, coordination and oversight.**

While reading Article 64 of the EU AI Act, one thing stood out to me: effective AI regulation depends heavily on having the right institutional capacity behind it.

Article 64 focuses on the AI Office, which plays an important role in strengthening AI expertise and capabilities at EU level.

In simple terms:

- The European Commission develops EU-level AI expertise and capabilities through the AI Office.
- Member States are expected to support the AI Office in carrying out the responsibilities given to it under the Regulation.
- This creates an important link between EU-level AI governance and national-level implementation.

For me, the key message is simple:

AI governance cannot work effectively in isolation.

Strong regulation needs coordination, shared expertise and cooperation between institutions.

Having rules is one thing. Building the capacity to implement and oversee those rules consistently is just as important.

Article 64 reminds us that responsible AI governance is not only about what the law says, but also about having the structures and expertise needed to make it work in practice.

> **Question for the reader:** What do you think? Will strong coordination between the AI Office and Member States be key to successful implementation of the EU AI Act?
