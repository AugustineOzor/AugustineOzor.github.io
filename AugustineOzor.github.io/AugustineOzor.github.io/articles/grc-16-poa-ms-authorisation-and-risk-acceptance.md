Assessments almost always leave some weaknesses open. The RMF handles them through two linked tools.

## The plan of action and milestones (POA&M)

A POA&M lists each open weakness with:

- a description and its source (assessment, scan, audit)
- its risk rating
- the planned remediation and the resources needed
- **milestones** and a target completion date
- a named owner and current status

A POA&M is a living document. Overdue items should be escalated, and closed items should have evidence attached.

## The authorisation package and decision

The **authorisation package** brings together the system security plan, the security assessment report and the POA&M. The **authorising official (AO)**, a senior leader with the authority to accept risk on the organisation's behalf, reviews it and decides:

- **Authorise to operate (ATO):** the residual risk is acceptable, usually with conditions and a review date.
- **Deny:** the risk is too high; the system must not operate until weaknesses are addressed.

## Accepting risk responsibly

When the AO accepts a risk rather than requiring a fix, that decision should be explicit and documented, with compensating controls and a review date. This is what makes accountability real: a named person knowingly owns the remaining risk.

**Key takeaway:** Track every open weakness in the POA&M, and make sure the person accepting the risk has both the authority and the full picture.
