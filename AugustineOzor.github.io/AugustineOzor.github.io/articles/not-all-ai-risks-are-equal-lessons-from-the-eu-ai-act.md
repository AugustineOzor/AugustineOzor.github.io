One of the most important ideas in AI governance is **proportionality**.

Simply put:

**Not all AI systems create the same level of risk, so they should not be governed the same way.**

The **EU AI Act** applies a risk-based approach that classifies AI systems according to their potential impact.

### **🟢 Minimal Risk**

These are generally low-impact applications such as:

✅ Spam filters
 ✅ AI-enabled games

They typically have limited regulatory obligations.

### **🟡 Limited Risk**

These systems require greater transparency.

For example:

✅ Chatbots
 ✅ AI systems that interact directly with users

Users may need to be informed that they are interacting with AI.

### **🟠 High Risk**

These systems can significantly affect people's rights, safety, or opportunities.

Examples include AI used in:

🔹 Employment and hiring
 🔹 Education
 🔹 Healthcare
 🔹 Credit and insurance
 🔹 Critical infrastructure
 🔹 Law enforcement
 🔹 Border control
 🔹 Justice systems

These systems require stronger controls, oversight, documentation, testing, and monitoring.

### **🔴 Unacceptable Risk**

These are AI uses considered incompatible with fundamental rights or public safety.

Examples include certain:

❌ Manipulative practices
 ❌ Exploitative systems
 ❌ Prohibited AI applications

These systems are not allowed under the Act.

### **Why This Matters**

The key question is not:

**"Is this AI system risky?"**

The better question is:

**"How risky is it, and what level of governance is appropriate?"**

Risk depends on factors such as:

✅ Intended use
 ✅ Data being processed
 ✅ Level of autonomy
 ✅ People affected
 ✅ Potential impact of decisions

### **Key Takeaway**

**Good AI governance scales with risk.**

The higher the potential impact on people, organizations, or society, the stronger the governance should be.

A one-size-fits-all approach rarely works.

**The right governance is proportional governance.**
