No governance framework is perfect.

In the real world, there will be situations where an AI use case cannot fully meet a policy, control, assessment, or timeline requirement.

The solution is **not** to ignore the gap.

The solution is to manage it through a formal exception process.

### **What Should an AI Exception Include?**

A proper exception should clearly document:

✅ Why the exception is needed
 ✅ Which AI system is affected
 ✅ The scope and duration
 ✅ The risk level
 ✅ Compensating controls
 ✅ The accountable owner
 ✅ The approving authority

### **Who Approves the Risk?**

Exceptions should never be approved in isolation.

Relevant stakeholders need to review the request and decide whether to:

🔹 Approve the exception
 🔹 Reject the exception
 🔹 Escalate the residual risk

### **Evidence Matters**

A mature exception process should maintain:

✅ An exception register
 ✅ Approval records
 ✅ Evidence of compensating controls
 ✅ Review and renewal dates
 ✅ Closure status

### **Why This Matters**

Many governance failures don't happen because policies are missing.

They happen because exceptions are handled informally, undocumented, or without clear accountability.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Good governance is not about eliminating flexibility. It's about managing flexibility responsibly.**

Controlled exceptions allow innovation to move forward while ensuring risks remain visible, documented, and accountable.

**Unmanaged exceptions create governance gaps. Managed exceptions create governance maturity.**
