# EU AI Act Article 57: A Safe Space to Test AI Before Going to Market

*By Augustine Ozor*

How do we encourage AI innovation without weakening regulatory oversight?

**Article 57 of the EU AI Act introduces one answer: AI Regulatory Sandboxes.**

These sandboxes provide a controlled environment where organizations can develop, train, test, and validate innovative AI systems under the supervision of competent authorities before going to market.

## What Does Article 57 Mean in Practice?

- **Test before launch:** Organizations can experiment with AI in a supervised environment before full deployment.
- **Work directly with regulators:** Authorities can provide guidance on regulatory requirements, testing, risk mitigation, and compliance.
- **Support innovation:** Sandboxes are designed to help organizations, particularly SMEs and start-ups, understand regulatory expectations without unnecessarily slowing innovation.
- **Learn through real testing:** The process can generate practical lessons for both organizations and regulators as AI technology evolves.

## An Important Point

A regulatory sandbox is not a compliance-free zone.

Organizations still need to follow the agreed sandbox plan, address identified risks, document results, and comply with applicable EU and national laws.

Article 57 shows that regulation and innovation do not have to work against each other.

The goal is to create a controlled space where organizations can:

Test. Learn. Improve. Comply.

Good AI regulation should not simply tell organizations what they cannot do.

It should also help them understand how to innovate responsibly.

> **Update (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force since July 2026):** National authorities now have until 2 August 2027 to establish at least one AI regulatory sandbox, and the AI Office may also create a sandbox at EU level. Check the consolidated Regulation for the current wording.

> **Question for the reader:** Do you think AI regulatory sandboxes can genuinely accelerate responsible innovation, or could the process become another regulatory hurdle?
