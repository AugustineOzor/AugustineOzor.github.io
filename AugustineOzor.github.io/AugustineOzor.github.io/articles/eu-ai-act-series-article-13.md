A well-designed AI system is useful only if deployers understand what it does, how it should be used and where its limitations lie. Article 13 makes transparency practical.

## Key Requirements

**Intended purpose**: Explain what the system is designed to do.

**Performance**: Communicate accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity information.

**Known risks**: Describe foreseeable risks and conditions affecting performance.

**Output interpretation**: Provide enough information to understand and use results appropriately.

**Human oversight**: Explain required review and intervention.

**Maintenance and resources**: Describe operational, technical and maintenance needs.

**Logging**: Explain available logging and monitoring capabilities.

## Why Article 13 Matters

Article 13 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

Hospital staff using an AI imaging tool should receive clear information on intended use, accuracy, limitations and required clinical review.

## Key Takeaway

**If users cannot understand a system well enough to use it responsibly, transparency has failed.**
