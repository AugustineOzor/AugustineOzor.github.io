Before an organization can manage AI risks, it needs to answer a simple question:

**What is the AI actually being used for?**

AI governance cannot happen in the abstract.

To govern AI effectively, organizations need to understand:

✅ What the system does
 ✅ Who uses it
 ✅ What data it processes
 ✅ Whether it supports or automates decisions
 ✅ Who could be affected by its outputs

Once the use case is clear, risks become easier to identify.

For example:

🔹 Bias and fairness issues
 🔹 Hallucinations and inaccurate outputs
 🔹 Data leakage risks
 🔹 Privacy concerns
 🔹 Security exposures
 🔹 Regulatory obligations
 🔹 Operational failures

Understanding use cases also helps organizations prioritize their efforts.

A high-impact AI system that influences hiring, lending, healthcare, or customer decisions requires much stronger governance than a low-risk productivity tool.

It also supports compliance by helping organizations determine which laws, regulations, policies, and contractual obligations apply.

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI governance begins with visibility.**

Before you can assess risk, implement controls, or demonstrate compliance, you first need to understand **what the AI is being used for and why it matters.**

The better you understand the use case, the better you can govern the AI.
