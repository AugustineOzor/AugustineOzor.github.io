As organizations increasingly adopt AI, having the right technology is only part of the equation. People also need the knowledge to use AI responsibly and effectively.

**Article 4 of the EU AI Act** introduces the concept of **AI Literacy**, requiring providers and deployers of AI systems to ensure that those working with AI have an appropriate level of understanding.

AI literacy includes understanding:

🔹 **What the AI system does**
 Knowing the purpose of the AI system, how it is used, and the tasks it supports.

🔹 **The benefits and limitations of AI**
 Understanding what AI can do well, where it may make mistakes, and when its outputs should be questioned.

🔹 **The risks associated with AI**
 Being aware of potential risks such as bias, inaccurate results, privacy concerns, or unintended impacts.

🔹 **When human oversight is needed**
 Recognizing that AI should support decision-making, not replace human judgment, especially for important decisions.

🔹 **How AI impacts people and decisions**
 Understanding how AI-driven outcomes can affect individuals, customers, employees, and other stakeholders.

The required level of AI literacy should be appropriate to a person's role, experience, and how they interact with the AI system.

**Example:**
 A bank uses AI to help assess loan applications. Employees using the system should understand how the AI supports decisions, its limitations, and when a decision requires human review.

**Key Takeaway:**
 AI literacy is not about making everyone an AI expert. It is about ensuring people have the right knowledge to use AI safely, responsibly, and with confidence.

**Trustworthy AI begins with AI-literate people.**
