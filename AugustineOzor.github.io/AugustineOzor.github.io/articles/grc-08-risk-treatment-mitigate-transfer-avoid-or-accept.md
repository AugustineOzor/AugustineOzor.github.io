Every assessed risk needs a response. There are four options.

- **Mitigate (reduce):** apply controls that lower likelihood or impact. Example: patch servers, enforce multi-factor authentication, encrypt the database.
- **Transfer (share):** move part of the financial impact to someone else, typically through cyber insurance or a contract with a service provider. Accountability does not transfer: if a vendor loses your patients' data, the obligations remain yours.
- **Avoid:** stop the activity that creates the risk. Example: decide not to store card numbers at all and use a payment provider instead.
- **Accept:** knowingly live with the risk because it falls within appetite or the cost of treatment outweighs the benefit.

## Accepting risk properly

Acceptance is a legitimate choice, but only when it is deliberate. A sound acceptance records the risk, the reason, the person with authority who approved it, any compensating controls and an expiry or review date. "We never got round to it" is not acceptance; it is neglect.

## Residual risk

Treatment rarely removes a risk completely. What remains is **residual risk**, and it should be compared with appetite again. If it is still too high, apply further treatment or escalate the decision.

**Key takeaway:** The right treatment depends on cost, appetite and context. What matters is that the decision is made by the right person and written down.
