**Responsible AI is moving from principles to practice.**

When we talk about fairness, transparency, accountability and human oversight, these are no longer simply ethical aspirations. They increasingly connect to governance, risk management, compliance and regulatory expectations.

This becomes clearer when we look across three important AI governance frameworks:

- **NIST AI RMF** focuses on trustworthy AI characteristics, stakeholder engagement, identifying potential harms, measuring risks and managing residual risk.
- **ISO/IEC 42001** helps organisations formalise responsible AI through policies, competence, risk treatment, impact assessment, transparency, operational controls and continual improvement.
- **The EU AI Act** turns several responsible AI principles into legal requirements through areas such as transparency, human oversight, prohibited practices, documentation, logging and information for users.

## Key takeaway

**Responsible AI cannot stop at having an ethics policy or a list of principles.**

Organisations need to be able to demonstrate how those principles are translated into controls, responsibilities, assessments, documentation, monitoring and evidence.

For example, saying an AI system is fair is a principle.
Documenting how fairness was assessed, who reviewed the results, what risks were identified and what actions were taken is governance.

That distinction matters.

As AI governance continues to mature, organisations will increasingly need to answer not only “Are we using AI responsibly?” but also “Can we demonstrate it?”
