Many organizations invest significant time creating AI policies.

But here's the real challenge:

**A policy that isn't maintained, reviewed, or enforced quickly loses its value.**

That's why effective AI governance requires more than policies. It requires **policy lifecycle management** and a **centralized policy register**.

### **🔹 The AI Policy Lifecycle**

AI policies should continuously move through a structured lifecycle:

✅ Define
 ✅ Approve
 ✅ Implement
 ✅ Monitor
 ✅ Review
 ✅ Update

Why is this important?

Because AI technologies, threats, regulations, and business use cases are constantly evolving. A policy written today may not fully address the risks that emerge tomorrow.

### **🔹 The Policy Register**

A policy register creates a **single source of truth** for AI governance.

It should capture:

✅ Policy owners
 ✅ Status and scope
 ✅ Review dates
 ✅ Related controls
 ✅ Approved exceptions
 ✅ Evidence and documentation

Without a central register, ownership becomes unclear and governance becomes difficult to monitor.

### **Why This Matters**

Organizations often focus on whether policies exist.

Auditors, regulators, and governance teams increasingly focus on whether policies are:

🔹 Current
 🔹 Owned
 🔹 Communicated
 🔹 Enforced
 🔹 Reviewed
 🔹 Supported by evidence

A policy sitting on a shared drive is not governance.

A policy that is actively managed, reviewed, and evidenced is.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Policy governance is a control in itself.**

Mature organizations must be able to demonstrate not only that AI policies exist, but that they are actively maintained and aligned with changing risks, regulations, and business needs.

**Good governance isn't just about writing policies. It's about proving they work.**
