Many organizations focus on deploying AI.

Fewer focus on understanding the risks AI introduces.

That's where **Pillar 2** comes in.

Its purpose is simple:

**Make AI risk visible before it becomes a business problem.**

### **1️⃣ Identify Where AI Creates Exposure**

Before managing AI risk, you need to understand where it exists.

This means looking beyond the model and understanding:

✅ The business process
 ✅ The data being used
 ✅ The users involved
 ✅ The decisions being made
 ✅ The stakeholders affected
 ✅ The operating environment

You can't manage a risk you haven't identified.

### **2️⃣ Prioritize Based on Impact**

Not all AI systems carry the same level of risk.

An internal productivity tool is very different from an AI system that impacts:

🔹 Employment
 🔹 Healthcare
 🔹 Financial decisions
 🔹 Safety
 🔹 Access to services
 🔹 Critical operations

The higher the impact, the stronger the governance should be.

### **3️⃣ Convert Risk Into Controls**

Identifying risk is only the beginning.

Organizations must implement controls that are appropriate for the use case, such as:

✅ Human oversight
 ✅ Data Loss Prevention (DLP)
 ✅ Testing and validation
 ✅ Monitoring and alerting
 ✅ Approval gates
 ✅ Vendor assessments
 ✅ Runtime restrictions

The goal is to reduce risk to an acceptable level.

### **4️⃣ Document Risk Decisions**

Good governance requires evidence.

Organizations should be able to show:

✅ Risks were assessed
 ✅ Controls were implemented
 ✅ Residual risks were understood
 ✅ Decisions were formally approved

If it isn't documented, it's difficult to demonstrate effective governance.

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI governance isn't about eliminating all risk. It's about making risk visible, understanding its impact, and managing it responsibly.**

The sooner organizations identify and address AI risks, the less likely those risks become business, compliance, or reputational issues later.

**Visibility leads to better decisions. Better decisions lead to better AI outcomes.**
