Having the right AI technology is only part of the equation. People also need enough knowledge to use AI safely and responsibly. Article 4 requires providers and deployers to take measures supporting an appropriate level of AI literacy.

## Key Requirements

**Purpose and use**: Understand what the AI system does and the tasks it supports.

**Benefits and limitations**: Know what the system does well and where it may fail.

**Risks**: Recognize bias, inaccurate output, privacy concerns and unintended impact.

**Human oversight**: Know when output must be questioned, escalated or overridden.

**Impact on people**: Understand how AI-supported outcomes can affect employees, customers and other stakeholders.

**Role-based learning**: Training should reflect a person’s role, experience and context.

## Why Article 4 Matters

Article 4 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

Employees using AI to support loan assessments should understand the model’s limitations, the data restrictions and when a decision requires human review.

## Key Takeaway

**AI literacy is not about making everyone an AI expert. It is about enabling informed and responsible use.**
