# EU AI Act Article 67: Who Gets a Voice in AI Governance?

*By Augustine Ozor*

AI governance should not be shaped by one group alone.

Article 67 of the EU AI Act establishes the Advisory Forum, creating a structured way for different stakeholders to contribute expertise to the implementation of the Act.

The Forum brings together voices from:

- Industry
- Start-ups and SMEs
- Civil society
- Academia

It also includes permanent participation from organisations with expertise in fundamental rights, cybersecurity and technical standards, including the Fundamental Rights Agency, ENISA, CEN, CENELEC and ETSI.

The Forum can provide opinions, recommendations and written contributions to the AI Board and European Commission. It can also establish specialist sub-groups to examine specific AI regulatory issues.

What makes Article 67 particularly important is the emphasis on balance.

AI regulation affects businesses, individuals, researchers, developers and society more broadly. Bringing these perspectives together can help ensure that implementation is informed not only by regulation, but also by technical expertise and real-world experience.

There is also a transparency element: the Forum prepares an annual report on its activities that is made publicly available.

For me, the message is simple:

Responsible AI governance benefits from diverse expertise, balanced participation and transparent decision-making.

Good AI governance is not just about who writes the rules. It is also about who has a voice when those rules are put into practice.

> **Question for the reader:** Do you think AI governance discussions currently include enough diverse perspectives?
