When people talk about AI, they often refer to it as if it's a single technology. There are different types of AI systems, each with its own capabilities, risks, and governance needs.

Understanding these differences is essential for effective AI governance and security.

🧠 **Generative AI**
 Creates content such as text, code, images, and videos. Examples include ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, and DALL·E.

🤖 **Agentic AI**
 Goes beyond generation by reasoning, planning, and taking actions to complete tasks. These systems can use tools, memory, and workflows to achieve objectives.

🧠 + 🤖 **Generative AI Agents**
 Combine content generation with the ability to take action. Examples include advanced copilots, AutoGPT, and AI-powered assistants that can create content, schedule meetings, or update systems.

🔁 **AI Agents**
 Operate behind the scenes to automate specific tasks such as querying APIs, summarizing logs, scanning emails, or enforcing policies with minimal human involvement.

### **Why It Matters**

Each type of AI introduces different risks and requires different controls:

🔹 Generative AI needs content governance and output monitoring.
 🔹 Agentic AI needs boundaries, permissions, and intent tracking.
 🔹 Generative AI Agents require both output and behavior monitoring.
 🔹 AI Agents need strong safeguards, logging, and oversight.

**Key Takeaway:**
Not all AI systems are the same. Understanding the type of AI you are using is the first step toward securing it, governing it, and using it responsibly.

**Effective AI governance starts with knowing what kind of AI you're governing.**
