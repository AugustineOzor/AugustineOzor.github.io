Many organizations are now familiar with AI governance frameworks such as **ISO/IEC 42001**, the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework**, and the **EU AI Act**.

But one question often comes up:

**How do we turn these frameworks into day-to-day governance practices?**

This is where a structured operating model becomes valuable.

A practical AI governance program can be organized around **five core pillars**:

### **1️⃣ Governance & Leadership**

Establishes accountability, decision-making authority, policies, and oversight.

Key question:

**Who is responsible for AI decisions and risks?**

### **2️⃣ Risk, Impact & Compliance**

Focuses on identifying, assessing, and managing AI-related risks, impacts, and regulatory obligations.

Key question:

**Have we understood and addressed the risks?**

### **3️⃣ Data & Model Lifecycle**

Governs AI systems from planning and development through deployment, monitoring, and retirement.

Key question:

**How are we managing data and models throughout their lifecycle?**

### **4️⃣ Secure Operations & Resilience**

Protects AI systems through monitoring, security controls, incident response, and resilience measures.

Key question:

**Can we operate AI safely in production?**

### **5️⃣ Ethics, Transparency & Acceptable Use**

Ensures AI is used responsibly, fairly, and transparently.

Key question:

**Can people trust how we use AI?**

### **Why This Matters**

AI governance is not one policy or one committee.

It requires an operating model that connects:

✅ Leadership
 ✅ Risk Management
 ✅ Data Governance
 ✅ Security
 ✅ Ethics

Frameworks provide the **"why"** and the **"what."**

An operating model provides the **"how."**

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI governance is most effective when it is operationalized.**

The organizations that succeed with AI will be those that move beyond principles and build repeatable practices across governance, risk, data, security, and ethics.

**Good AI governance isn't a document. It's an operating model.**
