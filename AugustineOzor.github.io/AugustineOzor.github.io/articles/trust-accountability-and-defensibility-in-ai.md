**Trusted AI is not just about building a good model. It is about being able to explain, govern and take responsibility for how it is used.**

One of the most important parts of AI governance is determining whether an AI system is trusted, defensible and appropriate for its intended context.

For me, this comes down to three things:

- **Trust:** People should understand how AI is being used, what it can and cannot do, what data may be involved and where human oversight remains.
- **Accountability:** Organisations need clear ownership of AI outcomes, acceptable use, disclosures, fairness reviews, harm mitigation and residual risk decisions.
- **Defensibility:** Fairness, transparency, documentation and human oversight are no longer simply ethical principles. They are increasingly becoming part of legal, regulatory and governance expectations.

## Key takeaway

**AI ethics and transparency cannot remain high-level principles written in a policy. They need to be operationalised.**

That means connecting them to owners, controls, requirements, approvals, monitoring and evidence.

Because when questions arise about an AI system, organisations should be able to explain not only what the AI did, but also who was responsible, what controls were applied and why the decision was considered acceptable.

That is where responsible AI becomes practical AI governance.
