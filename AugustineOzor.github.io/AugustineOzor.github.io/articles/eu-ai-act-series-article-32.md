Article 32 explains how relevant harmonised standards can support a presumption that a notified body meets corresponding Article 31 requirements.

## Key Requirements

**Harmonised standards**: Use standards whose references are published in the Official Journal.

**Presumption of conformity**: Covered requirements may benefit from the presumption.

**Limited scope**: The presumption applies only to what the standard actually covers.

**Gap evidence**: Uncovered requirements still require evidence.

**Consistency**: Standards can reduce duplication and improve consistency.

**Responsibility remains**: Using a standard is not a free pass.

## Why Article 32 Matters

Article 32 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A body relies on a harmonised standard for covered competence and quality requirements, then separately evidences AI-specific gaps.

## Key Takeaway

**Standards do the heavy lifting, but they do not remove responsibility.**
