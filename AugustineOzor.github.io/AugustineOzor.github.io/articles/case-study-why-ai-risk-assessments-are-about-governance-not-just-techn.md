Imagine a financial services company deploying a customer support chatbot.

At first glance, the chatbot may seem low risk. It answers questions, provides information, and directs customers to resources.

But what happens if it starts influencing:

🔹 Credit decisions
 🔹 Account access
 🔹 Fraud investigations
 🔹 Insurance outcomes
 🔹 Investment recommendations
 🔹 Customer complaints

The risk profile changes significantly.

That's why AI risk assessment should begin with a simple question:

**What is the AI actually being allowed to do?**

Using frameworks such as the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**, organizations can assess risk across:

✅ Governance and ownership
 ✅ Data access and quality
 ✅ Model behavior and limitations
 ✅ Users and affected stakeholders
 ✅ Business processes and operating context

From there, controls can be implemented, such as:

🔹 Prompt filtering
 🔹 Data Loss Prevention (DLP)
 🔹 Human escalation paths
 🔹 Monitoring and alerting
 🔹 Audit logging
 🔹 Scope restrictions

Once controls are in place, the remaining or **residual risk** should be documented and formally reviewed before deployment.

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI risk assessment is not a one-time technical review.**

It is an ongoing governance process that helps organizations:

✅ Define scope
 ✅ Classify risk
 ✅ Apply controls
 ✅ Document residual risk
 ✅ Reassess as systems evolve

A chatbot may start as a low-risk support tool, but governance is what ensures it stays within its approved role.

**Good AI governance starts with understanding the use case and continues throughout the AI lifecycle.**
