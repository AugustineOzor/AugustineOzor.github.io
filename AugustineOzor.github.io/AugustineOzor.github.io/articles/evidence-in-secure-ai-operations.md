**In AI security and governance, having controls is not enough. Organizations must be able to prove that those controls are working.**

Secure AI operations should produce clear, traceable evidence across the lifecycle.

### Prevention Evidence
Runtime configurations, IAM policies, DLP and gateway rules, approved tool lists, risk assessments, and deployment checklists demonstrate that preventive controls were established.

### Detection Evidence
Prompt and output logs, monitoring dashboards, anomaly alerts, misuse reports, and SOC tickets show that AI activity is being monitored and potential threats are being identified.

### Response Evidence
Incident records, escalation notes, containment actions, privacy or legal notifications, and incident timelines demonstrate how AI-related incidents were handled.

### Recovery Evidence
Rollback records, change tickets, continuity tests, model and prompt version history, and restored baseline records help demonstrate that AI systems were returned to a safe and governed state.

### Improvement Evidence
Lessons learned, updated policies, control tuning, training updates, and KPI reports show that organizations are learning from operational experience and strengthening their AI governance.

This evidence becomes important during audits, risk assessments, compliance reviews, incident investigations, and management reporting.

## Key takeaway

**If an organization cannot demonstrate that an AI control was implemented, monitored, tested, and improved, it becomes difficult to prove that the control is actually effective.**

Without evidence, secure AI operations are assumed. With evidence, they can be demonstrated.
