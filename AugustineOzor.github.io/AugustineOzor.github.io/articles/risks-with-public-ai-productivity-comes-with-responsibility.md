Public AI tools such as **ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and public Copilot experiences** are transforming how people work. They help us write faster, research more efficiently, and solve problems quickly.

But their convenience can also create significant risks when used without proper governance.

### **🔹 Shadow Input & Data Retention**

Employees often paste sensitive information into AI tools:

- Customer emails

- Contracts

- Internal reports

- Source code

The challenge is that organizations may not know where that data goes, how long it is retained, or how it is used, especially when employees use personal accounts.

### **🔹 Limited Access Controls**

Public AI is usually self-service.

Anyone can create an account, connect plugins, and start using AI in minutes.

Without enterprise governance, organizations have limited ability to:

✅ Control who uses AI
 ✅ Restrict access to sensitive information
 ✅ Enforce least-privilege principles

### **🔹 Untrusted or Unverifiable Outputs**

AI can generate impressive answers, but not always accurate ones.

Risks include:

- Hallucinations

- Outdated information

- Biased recommendations

- Incorrect code

- Intellectual property concerns

Without human review, inaccurate outputs can quickly spread into business processes and decisions.

### **🔹 Shadow AI and Data Loss**

One of the biggest concerns is the use of unmanaged AI tools.

When employees use free or unauthorized AI services:

🔹 Data may bypass organizational controls
 🔹 Activity may not be logged
 🔹 DLP and monitoring tools may not apply
 🔹 Incident investigations become more difficult

This can create an invisible pathway for data exposure.

### **Why This Matters**

Public AI is not inherently risky.

The real challenge is using it without visibility, governance, or clear rules.

Organizations can reduce risk through:

✅ Clear AI usage policies
 ✅ Employee awareness and training
 ✅ AI discovery and monitoring capabilities
 ✅ Approved enterprise AI solutions
 ✅ Human review of important outputs

### **Key Takeaway**

**Public AI is not secure by default.**

It delivers significant productivity benefits, but those benefits need to be balanced with governance, accountability, and data protection.

The goal is not to stop people from using AI.

The goal is to ensure they use it safely, responsibly, and in a way that protects both the organization and its data.
