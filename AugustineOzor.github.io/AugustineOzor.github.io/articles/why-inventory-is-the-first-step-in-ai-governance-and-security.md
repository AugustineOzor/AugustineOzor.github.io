Many organizations are focused on deploying AI, but fewer are asking a critical question:

**Do we actually know where AI is being used across the organization?**

Before you can govern, secure, monitor, or assess AI risks, you need visibility.

That starts with an **AI inventory**.

An effective AI inventory goes beyond simply listing tools. It helps organizations understand:

🔹 **What AI systems are being used**
 Public AI, Embedded AI, or Homegrown AI?

🔹 **Who owns them**
 Is there a business owner, system owner, and governance accountability?

🔹 **What data they access**
 Are employees entering sensitive information into prompts? Are RAG systems accessing regulated data?

🔹 **How they behave**
 Do they generate content, support decisions, automate workflows, or take actions?

Why does this matter?

Because not all AI systems create the same risks.

A public GenAI tool used for writing assistance presents different challenges than an embedded AI feature in a SaaS platform or an internally developed AI agent connected to enterprise systems.

Without an accurate inventory:

❌ You cannot assess risk effectively
 ❌ You cannot apply appropriate controls
 ❌ You cannot monitor usage or incidents
 ❌ You cannot demonstrate governance or compliance

Whether you're following the **EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, or internal governance requirements**, inventory is the foundation everything else is built on.

### **Key Takeaway**

**You can't govern what you haven't discovered.**

Visibility is not just the first step in AI governance and security. It's the prerequisite for everything that comes after.
