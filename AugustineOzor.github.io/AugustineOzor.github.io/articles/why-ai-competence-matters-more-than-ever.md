Many organizations are investing in AI policies, governance frameworks, and controls.

But there's one challenge that often gets overlooked:

**Governance fails when people don't understand their responsibilities.**

AI governance is not just an IT issue. Every part of the organization has a role to play.

🔹 **Leaders** need to understand accountability, decision-making, risk tolerance, and when to escalate concerns.

🔹 **Business users** need to know which AI tools are approved, how to handle data responsibly, and when human review is required.

🔹 **Technical teams** need expertise in data quality, testing, model risk, security, monitoring, and change management.

🔹 **Control functions** such as Legal, Privacy, Risk, Compliance, and Security need to understand AI obligations and evidence requirements.

The reality is simple:

✅ Policies provide direction.
 ✅ Technology provides capability.
 ✅ People make governance work.

That's why AI competence and awareness programs are becoming a critical part of effective AI governance. They help turn policies into informed decisions and responsible behaviors across the organization.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Successful AI governance depends as much on people as it does on technology and policies.**

When employees understand their roles, organizations are better positioned to manage risk, build trust, and realize the full value of AI.
