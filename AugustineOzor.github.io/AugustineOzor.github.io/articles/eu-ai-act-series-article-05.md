Not every AI use is acceptable. Article 5 establishes red lines for practices considered to create unacceptable risks to rights, safety or human dignity.

## Key Requirements

**Manipulation and deception**: Certain harmful manipulative or deceptive practices are prohibited.

**Exploitation of vulnerability**: AI must not exploit specified vulnerabilities in ways likely to cause significant harm.

**Social scoring**: Certain social-scoring practices are prohibited.

**Facial-image scraping**: Untargeted scraping to create or expand facial-recognition databases is prohibited.

**Emotion recognition and biometric categorization**: Certain uses are prohibited, subject to the exact legal conditions.

**Remote biometric identification**: Certain real-time law-enforcement uses in public spaces are generally prohibited, with narrowly defined conditions and exceptions.

## Why Article 5 Matters

Article 5 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A workplace tool claiming to infer employee emotions from video should be screened before procurement. The exact intended purpose and legal conditions matter.

## Key Takeaway

**Responsible AI begins with knowing what should not be built, bought or deployed.**
