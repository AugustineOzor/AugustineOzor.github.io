**AI ethics becomes useful when we can turn principles into practice.**

Many organizations already have AI principles around fairness, transparency and accountability.

The challenge is making those principles measurable, consistent and actionable.

This is where frameworks such as the OECD AI Principles can help.

The OECD AI Principles provide a useful foundation for responsible AI, covering areas such as:

- Human-centred values and fairness
- Transparency and explainability
- Robustness, security and safety
- Accountability
- Inclusive growth and well-being

But principles alone are not enough.

Organizations need to translate them into policies, responsibilities, measurable requirements, controls and evidence.

For example, if fairness is a principle, the next questions should be:

How will fairness be measured? Who reviews the results? What happens when acceptable thresholds are not met? Who can accept the remaining risk?

This is where many AI ethics programs struggle. Values may be clear, but policies can remain vague, controls fragmented and accountability unclear.

## Key takeaway

**Frameworks provide direction. Governance turns that direction into action.**

Responsible AI becomes more meaningful when ethical principles can be translated into measurable requirements, enforceable controls and evidence that shows those controls are working.
