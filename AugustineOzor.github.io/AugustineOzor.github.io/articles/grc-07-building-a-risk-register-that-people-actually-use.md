A risk register is the working record of an organisation's risks. A good one is short enough to read, specific enough to act on, and reviewed often.

## What each entry needs

- A clear **risk statement** (threat, vulnerability, asset, impact)
- **Likelihood** and **impact** ratings, and the resulting score
- A named **risk owner** who is accountable for it
- The **treatment** decision and the controls that support it
- **Residual risk** after treatment, a **due date** and the **status**

## Scoring

Most programmes use a **5×5 matrix**: likelihood and impact each rated 1 to 5, multiplied for a score out of 25. Define what each level means in your context, for example "impact 5 = harm to patients or regulatory sanction", so different assessors rate consistently.

## Risk appetite

Scores only become decisions when compared with **risk appetite**: how much risk leadership is willing to accept to achieve its goals. A healthcare provider handling patient data will usually have a low appetite for confidentiality risk, so a moderate score there may still demand action.

## Keeping it alive

Review the register on a fixed cadence, update it when systems or threats change, and report the top risks to leadership in plain language. A register reviewed only at audit time is a compliance artefact, not a management tool.

**Key takeaway:** Every risk needs a score, an owner and a decision. Without all three, it is just a worry on a list.
