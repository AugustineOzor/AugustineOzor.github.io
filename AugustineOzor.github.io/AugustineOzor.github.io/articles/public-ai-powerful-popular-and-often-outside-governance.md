When most people think about AI, they think about tools like **ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and public Copilot experiences**.

These tools are incredibly useful. They help with writing, research, coding, analysis, and productivity.

But they also introduce a challenge for organizations:

**AI adoption often happens faster than governance.**

### **What Makes Public AI Different?**

Unlike traditional enterprise applications, Public AI tools are often accessed through:

🔹 Personal accounts
 🔹 Browser-based applications
 🔹 Mobile apps
 🔹 Plugins and extensions

This means they frequently operate **outside normal enterprise visibility and control.**

### **Three Important Questions**

#### **🔍 Can you see where your data goes?**

Often, the answer is **not completely**.

Employees may paste documents, emails, customer information, or business data directly into an AI tool with limited organizational visibility into how that information is processed or retained.

#### **🔐 Can you control who has access?**

Usually, **not easily**.

Many Public AI services are self-service. Users can create accounts and begin using AI without formal onboarding, approval, or governance processes.

#### **📋 Can you audit what was entered or generated?**

In many organizations, **no**.

Without logging, monitoring, or approved enterprise deployments, it can be difficult to determine what information was shared or what outputs influenced business decisions.

### **Why This Matters**

Public AI creates one of the most common forms of **Shadow AI**.

The risk is often not the technology itself, but the lack of visibility, governance, and controls around its use.

### **Practical Ways Organizations Can Reduce Risk**

✅ Establish clear AI usage policies

✅ Provide employee awareness and training

✅ Discover and monitor AI usage

✅ Use approved enterprise AI solutions when available

✅ Encourage human review of AI-generated outputs

### **Key Takeaway**

**Public AI is not inherently unsafe, but it does require governance.**

The goal should not be to block innovation. The goal should be to enable employees to use AI responsibly while protecting sensitive data, maintaining compliance, and reducing risk.

**The organizations that succeed with AI will be the ones that balance innovation with visibility, accountability, and trust.**
