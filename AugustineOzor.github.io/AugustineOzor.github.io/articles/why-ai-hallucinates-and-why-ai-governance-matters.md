One of the biggest misconceptions about AI is that it "knows" things. It doesn't.

AI models are trained to **predict the next likely word or token** based on patterns learned from massive amounts of data. They don't understand facts the way humans do, and they don't verify information before responding.

This creates a challenge known as **hallucination**: when AI generates information that sounds convincing but is actually incorrect.

From an AI governance and security perspective, this is more than an accuracy issue.

🔹 An AI system may generate incorrect policies or procedures.
 🔹 A chatbot may provide misleading advice to customers.
 🔹 An AI assistant may reference non-existent sources or regulations.
 🔹 Employees may trust AI-generated outputs without proper verification.

The risk increases when AI-generated content is treated as fact without human review.

This is why effective AI governance is not just about deploying AI. It's about implementing the right controls:

✅ Human oversight
 ✅ Output validation
 ✅ Risk assessments
 ✅ User training and AI literacy
 ✅ Monitoring and continuous review

**Key Takeaway:**
 AI predicts. It does not verify.

Understanding how AI works and why hallucinations occur is essential for building AI systems that are secure, trustworthy, and used responsibly.

**Trustworthy AI requires verification, not blind trust.**
