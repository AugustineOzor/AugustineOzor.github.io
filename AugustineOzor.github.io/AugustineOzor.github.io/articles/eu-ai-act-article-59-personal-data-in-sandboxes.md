# EU AI Act Article 59: Can Personal Data Be Reused to Develop AI?

*By Augustine Ozor*

Sometimes, innovative AI systems need real-world data to be properly developed, trained, and tested. But when that involves personal data, the rules become much stricter.

Article 59 of the EU AI Act allows personal data that was lawfully collected for another purpose to be further processed within an AI regulatory sandbox, but only under specific conditions.

## What does this mean?

The AI project must serve a substantial public interest, such as:

- Public health and safety
- Environmental protection
- Energy sustainability
- Transport and critical infrastructure
- Public administration and services

And importantly:

- Personal data must be genuinely necessary.
- Anonymised or synthetic data should be used where they can achieve the same objective.
- Data must be processed in a secure and isolated environment.
- Access must be restricted to authorised people.
- Risks to individuals must be monitored and addressed.
- Processing must not negatively affect individuals or their data-protection rights.
- Appropriate records and technical documentation must be maintained.
- Personal data must be deleted when it is no longer required under the applicable conditions.

## Why this matters

Article 59 tries to balance two important goals: innovation and privacy.

It recognises that real-world data can sometimes be essential for developing AI that serves the public interest, while making it clear that a regulatory sandbox is not a shortcut around data-protection rules.

## Key takeaway

Access to personal data for AI experimentation comes with responsibility. Use it only when necessary. Protect it carefully. Document the process. Delete it when required.

Innovation should not come at the expense of privacy.
