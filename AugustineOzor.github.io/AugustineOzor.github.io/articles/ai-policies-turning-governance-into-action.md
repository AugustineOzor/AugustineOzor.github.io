Many organizations talk about AI governance. But governance only becomes effective when it is translated into clear policies that people can follow, enforce, and audit.

Policies help transform high-level principles into practical rules and expectations across the organization.

### **🔹 1. Acceptable Use Policy**

Defines:

✅ Approved AI use cases
 ✅ Restricted uses
 ✅ Prohibited activities

This is often the first policy organizations create because it establishes the boundaries for AI use.

### **🔹 2. AI Risk Assessment Policy**

Defines when AI systems require:

✅ Risk reviews
 ✅ Classification
 ✅ Formal approval

Not every AI use case carries the same level of risk. This policy helps organizations apply the right level of oversight.

### **🔹 3. Data Protection / DPIA Policy**

Defines requirements for:

✅ Privacy assessments
 ✅ Data handling reviews
 ✅ Rights impact assessments
 ✅ Sensitive data protection

AI governance starts with good data governance.

### **🔹 4. Model Approval Policy**

Defines expectations for:

✅ Testing
 ✅ Validation
 ✅ Documentation
 ✅ Sign-off procedures

Before a model is deployed, organizations should know it is fit for purpose and appropriately reviewed.

### **🔹 5. Third-Party AI Policy**

Defines requirements for:

✅ Vendor due diligence
 ✅ Third-party risk reviews
 ✅ Contractual oversight
 ✅ Ongoing monitoring

When using external AI providers, responsibility does not disappear.

### **🔹 6. AI Incident Response Policy**

Defines how organizations:

✅ Escalate AI-related issues
 ✅ Investigate failures
 ✅ Manage incidents
 ✅ Implement corrective actions

Because AI incidents require a response process that extends beyond traditional IT incidents.

### **Why This Matters**

Policies create clarity.

They help teams answer questions such as:

- What AI is allowed?

- When is approval required?

- Who owns the risk?

- How should incidents be handled?

- What evidence needs to be retained?

Without policies, governance often becomes inconsistent and difficult to enforce.

### **Key Takeaway**

**AI policies are where governance becomes operational.**

They establish accountability, define expectations, and create the foundation for lifecycle controls, monitoring, compliance, and enforcement.

Good AI governance is not just about principles.

**It's about creating clear rules that people can actually follow.**
