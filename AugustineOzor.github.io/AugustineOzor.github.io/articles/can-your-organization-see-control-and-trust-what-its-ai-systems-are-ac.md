Traditional security tools were designed for networks, endpoints, and applications.

AI introduces new challenges:

• Models make decisions
 • Agents take actions
 • Prompts become inputs
 • Outputs become business decisions
 • Data flows through training sets, RAG pipelines, memory, plugins, and connectors

Securing AI requires capabilities that understand AI behavior, not just IT infrastructure.

A practical AI security control plane includes:

🔹 **Discover & Govern**

- Data Security & Posture Management (DSPM)

- AI Security & Posture Management (AISPM)

Know where sensitive data exists, which AI systems are being used, and who owns them.

🔹 **Monitor & Protect**

- Identity & Access Management (IAM)

- AI Firewalls, DLP & Gateways

- AI Detection & Response

Control who and what can interact with AI, detect misuse, and stop prompt injection, data leakage, and unsafe actions.

🔹 **Recover & Trust**

- Audit Trail & Lineage

- Data Integrity & Provenance

- AI Red Teaming

Maintain evidence, validate data sources, and continuously test AI systems against adversarial attacks.

The organizations that succeed with AI won't be the ones deploying the most models.

They'll be the ones that can answer these questions:

✅ What AI systems do we have?
 ✅ What data do they use?
 ✅ What are they allowed to do?
 ✅ How do we know they're behaving as expected?
 ✅ What evidence proves it?

**AI security is no longer just about protecting systems. It's about governing intelligent behavior.**
