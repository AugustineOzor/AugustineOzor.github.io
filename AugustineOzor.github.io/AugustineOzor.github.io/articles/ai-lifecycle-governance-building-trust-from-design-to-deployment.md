One of the biggest misconceptions about AI governance is that it's a single approval before deployment.

It isn't.

**Effective AI governance follows the entire AI lifecycle.**

### **Stage 1: Planning & Design**

Before any model is built, organizations should ask:

✅ What are we building?
 ✅ Why are we building it?
 ✅ Who could be affected?

A marketing content assistant and an AI system influencing healthcare or hiring decisions have very different risk profiles.

Good governance starts with understanding the use case, stakeholders, and potential impacts.

### **Stage 2: Data Collection & Processing**

AI systems depend on data.

Organizations need to ensure that data is:

✅ Lawful to use
 ✅ Properly classified
 ✅ High quality
 ✅ Relevant to the use case
 ✅ Traceable through lineage

A key principle:

**Data availability does not equal data usability.**

### **Stage 3: Build or Select Model**

Whether you're building, buying, fine-tuning, or integrating a model, governance still applies.

Organizations should review:

✅ Model ownership
 ✅ Data sources
 ✅ Vendor dependencies
 ✅ Security controls
 ✅ Known limitations

### **Stage 4: Testing, Evaluation, Verification & Validation (TEVV)**

Before deployment, AI systems should be tested for more than accuracy.

This includes:

✅ Bias and fairness
 ✅ Security and privacy
 ✅ Robustness
 ✅ Hallucinations and unintended behavior
 ✅ Real-world performance

The goal is simple:

**Can we justify deployment?**

### **Why This Matters**

AI governance becomes much more effective when viewed as a lifecycle rather than a one-time approval.

Every stage creates new decisions, risks, controls, and evidence requirements.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Trustworthy AI is built through governance at every stage of the lifecycle, not just at deployment.**

The earlier governance starts, the easier it becomes to manage risk, demonstrate compliance, and build trust.
