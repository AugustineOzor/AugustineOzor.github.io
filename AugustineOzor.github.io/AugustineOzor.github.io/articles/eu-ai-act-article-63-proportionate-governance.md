# EU AI Act Article 63: A Good Example of Proportionate AI Governance

*By Augustine Ozor*

**EU AI Act Article 63 is a good example of proportionate AI governance.**

The idea is straightforward: certain eligible microenterprises can take a simplified approach to elements of the quality management system required under Article 17.

What Article 63 does not do is remove the broader responsibilities attached to high-risk AI systems.

## Organizations still need to think about

- AI risk management
- Data governance
- Technical documentation
- Logging and record-keeping
- Transparency
- Human oversight
- Accuracy and robustness
- Cybersecurity
- Post-market monitoring
- Serious incident reporting

From a GRC perspective, this distinction is important.

AI governance frameworks should be proportionate enough to support innovation but strong enough to protect people and maintain accountability.

A smaller organization may need simpler documentation, fewer layers of approval and a more practical quality management structure.

But the objective remains the same: safe, trustworthy and compliant AI.

Article 63 reminds us that good AI governance isn't necessarily about creating more paperwork. It's about having the right controls, evidence and accountability for the level of risk involved.

**Lighter administration should never mean lighter accountability.**

> **Update (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force since July 2026):** The Omnibus extended the simplified quality management option in Article 63 to small mid-cap enterprises (not SMEs, fewer than 750 employees and turnover up to EUR 150 million, or a qualifying balance sheet total). The article's reach is therefore wider than microenterprises alone. Check the consolidated Regulation for the current wording.
