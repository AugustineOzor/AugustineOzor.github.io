**You can’t govern AI systems you can’t see.**

One of the growing challenges in AI governance is detecting misuse after AI systems move into real-world environments.

Many organizations still have limited visibility into how AI is actually being used. Prompts, outputs, tool calls, automated actions, and user behavior may occur outside traditional monitoring systems, creating significant blind spots.

This matters because AI misuse can take many forms:

- Sensitive data leakage through prompts or outputs
- Prompt injection and manipulation
- Unsafe or unauthorized AI-generated content
- AI agents performing unintended actions
- Employees using unapproved AI tools or models
- Activity that violates organizational AI policies

Traditional security logs alone may not provide enough context to explain what happened, why it happened, and what the AI system actually did.

Organizations need AI-specific monitoring that captures the right signals across the AI lifecycle, while integrating them into existing SOC and incident response workflows.

But more alerts are not necessarily better alerts.

The goal should be high-signal detection, with sufficient context for analysts to understand the prompt, response, action, user, system, and applicable policy, supported by clearly defined escalation and response paths.

## Key takeaway

**Monitor AI before misuse becomes an incident.**

Effective AI governance is not only about policies and risk assessments. It also requires operational visibility into how AI systems behave once they are deployed and used.
