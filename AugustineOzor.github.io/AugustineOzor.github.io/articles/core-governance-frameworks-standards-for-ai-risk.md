As AI adoption grows, many organizations focus on controls, tools, and technologies. But one important question remains:

**How do we know we're managing AI risk effectively?**

That's where governance frameworks come in.

Frameworks provide a structured approach for managing AI risks, assigning accountability, and demonstrating compliance. They help organizations answer two critical questions:

✅ What should we be doing?
 ✅ How do we prove we're doing it?

Here are four of the most important AI governance frameworks and standards organizations should understand:

### **🔹 NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**

A practical framework for identifying, assessing, and managing AI risks.

It focuses on four key functions:

- **Govern** – Establish accountability and oversight

- **Map** – Understand context, purpose, and impacts

- **Measure** – Assess and evaluate risks

- **Manage** – Implement controls and mitigations

### **🔹 ISO/IEC 42001**

The world's first AI Management System standard.

Similar to ISO 27001 for information security, it provides guidance on:

- AI governance

- Risk management

- Data governance

- Roles and responsibilities

- Continuous improvement

- Incident management

### **🔹 OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications**

A security-focused framework that highlights common risks affecting generative AI systems, including:

- Prompt injection

- Data leakage

- Insecure integrations

- Model misuse

- Supply chain vulnerabilities

An important resource for security professionals and AI developers.

### **🔹 EU AI Act**

The European Union's AI regulation introduces a risk-based approach to AI governance.

It requires organizations to address areas such as:

- Risk management

- Documentation

- Transparency

- Human oversight

- Accountability for high-risk AI systems

Its impact extends beyond Europe and may affect any organization whose AI systems interact with the EU market.

### **Why This Matters**

Controls protect AI systems.

**Frameworks protect organizations.**

They provide a common language for governance, help align security and compliance efforts, and reduce the risk of regulatory surprises, costly remediation, and reputational damage.

### **Key Takeaway**

**Successful AI governance is not built on technology alone. It's built on strong frameworks, clear accountability, and consistent processes.**

The earlier organizations align with recognized standards and frameworks, the better prepared they will be to manage AI risks responsibly and at scale.

Controls protect the system. Frameworks protect the organization.
