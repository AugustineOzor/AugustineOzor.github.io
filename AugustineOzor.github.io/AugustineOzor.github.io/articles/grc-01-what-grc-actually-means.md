GRC stands for **governance, risk and compliance**. The three are often listed together, but each answers a different question.

**Governance** asks: *who decides, and how?* It sets direction through strategy, policies, roles and oversight, so security decisions reflect what the organisation is trying to achieve.

**Risk management** asks: *what could go wrong, and how much does it matter?* It identifies threats to the things the organisation values, estimates how likely and how damaging they are, and decides what to do about them.

**Compliance** asks: *are we meeting our obligations?* Those obligations come from laws and regulations (such as HIPAA or the EU AI Act), contracts (such as PCI DSS for card payments) and internal policy.

## Why they belong together

Each discipline fails on its own. Governance without risk management produces policies that protect the wrong things. Risk management without governance has no authority to act. Compliance without either becomes a paperwork exercise that passes audits while real risks grow.

Together they form a loop: governance sets the rules and risk appetite, risk management decides where to focus, controls are put in place, and compliance checks and proves that those controls work.

## Example

A hospital runs an electronic medical record system such as OpenMRS. Governance assigns a system owner and approves a data protection policy. Risk management finds that unpatched servers could expose patient records. Compliance confirms that the resulting controls (patching, encryption, access reviews) meet HIPAA's Security Rule and keeps the evidence.

**Key takeaway:** GRC is not three departments. It is one way of making security decisions that are deliberate, risk-based and provable.
