AI changes quickly. Article 7 supports updating high-risk use cases as technology, deployment and evidence of harm evolve.

## Key Requirements

**Purpose**: What the system is designed to do and where it is used.

**Scale**: How widely it is used and how many people may be affected.

**Data**: Whether sensitive or personal information is involved.

**Autonomy and oversight**: How independently the system operates and whether humans can intervene.

**Evidence of harm**: Incidents, complaints, reports and other evidence.

**Reversibility**: Whether outcomes can be corrected or reversed.

## Why Article 7 Matters

Article 7 helps translate the EU AI Act into clearer expectations for accountability, evidence, oversight and responsible operation. The requirement matters because AI governance is credible only when responsibilities and controls are understood and applied in practice.

## Real-World Example

A small AI use may become widely adopted in employment or healthcare. If later evidence shows significant risk, classification expectations may change.

## Key Takeaway

**As AI changes, governance and classification must change with it.**
