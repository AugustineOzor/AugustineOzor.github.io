One of the reasons modern AI feels so intelligent is because it can access information that is specific to your organization. This is often made possible through **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**.

A RAG index acts like a knowledge repository for AI. It pulls content from sources such as SharePoint, databases, file shares, data lakes, and business applications, allowing the AI to retrieve relevant information before generating a response.

This is what transforms a general-purpose AI model into an assistant that understands your business.

But from a security perspective, this creates a critical challenge.

The RAG index often contains some of the organization's most valuable information:

🔹 Customer records
 🔹 Financial data
 🔹 Internal policies and procedures
 🔹 Business strategies
 🔹 Contracts and intellectual property

If access controls are weak or the index is poorly governed, AI may expose information to users who should not have access to it.

To reduce this risk, organizations should focus on:

🔹 **Data Classification**
 Know exactly what information is being added to the index.

🔹 **Data Integrity & Provenance**
 Track where information comes from and ensure it is accurate and trustworthy.

🔹 **Access Controls**
 Ensure users only access information they are authorized to see.

🔹 **Logging & Monitoring**
 Record queries and retrieved content to detect misuse and investigate incidents.

🔹 **Continuous Governance**
 Treat the RAG index as a high-value data asset, not just an AI feature.

### **Key Takeaway**

The RAG index is what makes AI useful and context-aware. But the more knowledge it contains, the more important it becomes to secure and govern it properly.

**A smart AI is only as secure as the data it can access.**
