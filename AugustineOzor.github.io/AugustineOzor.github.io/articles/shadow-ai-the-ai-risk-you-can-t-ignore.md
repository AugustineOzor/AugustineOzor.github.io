Many organizations still think of AI as a single project or platform. The reality is very different.

Today, AI is **distributed across the organization**. From internal copilots and AI assistants to chatbots, analytics platforms, and SaaS tools, the average organization is using multiple AI solutions, often across different teams and use cases.

Each tool interacts with different data, users, and environments, creating unique security, privacy, and compliance risks.

The challenge? **Many organizations don't have full visibility into what AI tools are actually being used.**

This is known as **Shadow AI**: AI tools adopted without formal approval, oversight, or monitoring.

Examples include:

🔹 Employees using public AI tools with sensitive company data
 🔹 Browser extensions and AI plugins that haven't been reviewed
 🔹 External AI services storing prompts and business information
 🔹 AI tools with persistent memory retaining more data than expected

The risk is simple:

**You can't secure what you don't know exists.**

Before organizations can govern AI effectively, protect sensitive data, or apply appropriate controls, they need visibility into their AI landscape.

**Strong AI governance starts with knowing where AI is being used.**
