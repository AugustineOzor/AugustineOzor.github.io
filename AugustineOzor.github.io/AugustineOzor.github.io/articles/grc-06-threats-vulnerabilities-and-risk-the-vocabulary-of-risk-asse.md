Risk conversations go wrong when people use the same words for different things. These definitions keep an assessment precise.

- **Asset:** anything of value that needs protecting, such as a database of patient records, a server or a business process.
- **Threat:** something that could cause harm, such as a ransomware group, a careless insider or a power failure.
- **Vulnerability:** a weakness a threat could exploit, such as unpatched software, excessive user privileges or missing staff training.
- **Likelihood:** how probable it is that a threat exploits a vulnerability.
- **Impact:** how much damage would result: financial, operational, legal or reputational.
- **Risk:** the combination of likelihood and impact for a specific threat exploiting a specific vulnerability against an asset.

## Putting it together

A risk statement links them: *"Ransomware (threat) could exploit unpatched servers (vulnerability) to encrypt the patient record database (asset), disrupting clinical care and triggering breach notification (impact)."*

Writing risks this way keeps them specific enough to act on. "Cyber attack" is not a risk; it is a category.

## Example

For a museum's IT estate, three common pairs are ransomware exploiting outdated ticketing systems, insiders exploiting excessive access to donor records, and phishing exploiting a lack of awareness training. Each becomes its own line in the risk register, with its own owner and treatment.

**Key takeaway:** A risk is not a threat or a vulnerability. It is what happens when they meet something you care about.
